Babysitter hourly rates by city (2026)

Updated September 8, 2026 · 5 min read

"What do I pay the sitter?" has gotten more expensive to answer. Babysitting rates rose again in the past year, outpacing inflation. Here's what's fair to pay in 2026 — nationally, by city, and by how many kids you're leaving behind.

What is the average babysitting rate in 2026?

UrbanSitter’s 2026 booking data reports $26.24/hour for one child and $29.87/hour for two. These are averages from that platform, not a census of every sitter or a required rate. Agree on a price for your location, schedule and responsibilities before the booking.

What to pay for 3, 4 or 5 hours

At an agreed $20/hour for the whole booking, 3 hours costs $60, 4 hours costs $80, and 5 hours costs $100. Four and a half hours costs $90. These are arithmetic examples, not suggested market rates. If your agreed rate already includes two children, do not add another child surcharge.

What raises the rate

  • More kids: add about $2–$5/hour per additional child.
  • Ages: infants and multiple young children pay more than one school-age child.
  • Overnights & holidays: expect a premium; New Year's Eve is the classic surge night.
  • Extras: cooking, driving, bath-and-bed, or light tidying nudge it up.
  • Last-minute or late-night: short notice earns a few dollars more.

Sitter vs. nanny — why the rates differ

A babysitter usually covers occasional care; a nanny typically has an ongoing role. Neither arrangement guarantees a higher or lower hourly price. Compare responsibilities, experience, hours and benefits. See nanny pay planning and check the applicable employment rules separately.
